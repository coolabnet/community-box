# 5.1 What we mean by AI

In this report, AI means three kinds of tools:

- **Chat assistants.** You type a question or request, and the tool writes an answer. For example, drafting a letter or explaining a form.
- **Voice tools.** You speak, and the tool listens, answers, or writes down what you said.
- **Translation.** The tool turns text or speech from one language into another.

There are two ways to use these tools.

- **Online services.** A company runs the tool on its own computers. You use it through a website or app, and you need an internet connection. Your text goes to the company's servers.
- **Small models on the Community Box.** A smaller AI model runs on hardware that the community owns. Your text can stay on the local machine, but only if the whole setup is configured and checked to stay local. It can work without the internet. Its answers may be less detailed than those of large online services.

For hardware, this difference matters. An online service needs only a connection and a browser. A local model needs much more memory and processing power, which is why the Community Box must be sized for it. Online services also send data out of the community, so the community must decide what it is willing to share.
