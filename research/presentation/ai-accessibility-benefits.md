# 5.2 Accessibility Benefits

AI tools can help community members who face barriers to using computers and the internet.

- **Low digital literacy.** Someone who does not know how to use a computer can ask a voice or chat tool for help instead. For example, a farmer can ask how to fill in a government form, step by step.
- **Limited traditional literacy.** Someone who cannot read or write well can speak to the tool and hear the answer read aloud. This helps people who never learned to read but still need information.
- **Voice-based interaction.** Speaking is easier than typing for many people. A voice tool lets an elder or a person with a disability use the Community Box without a keyboard.

These tools do not replace people. A neighbor, a trainer, or a community helper is still needed to set things up, check answers, and explain what the tool said. The goal is to let more people take part, not to leave them alone with a machine.
